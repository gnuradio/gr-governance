# **GNU Radio Technical Governance**

## **1\. Purpose**

This document establishes the technical governance of the GNU Radio Project under the Linux Foundation.

The governance model is intended to provide continuity with the GNU Radio General Assembly established under the GNU Radio Articles of Association. Upon transition of GNU Radio's technical governance to the Linux Foundation, the General Assembly's responsibility for the technical direction and stewardship of the GNU Radio Project will continue through the **GNU Radio Technical Steering Committee (TSC)**.

The TSC is therefore intended to be a direct successor to the General Assembly for purposes of technical governance rather than a newly constituted technical body.

This document governs technical and community matters associated with the GNU Radio Project. Financial, corporate, contractual, conference, trademark, and other non-technical responsibilities may be governed separately by applicable Linux Foundation agreements, policies, committees, or other organizations.

## **3\. Technical Steering Committee**

The **Technical Steering Committee (TSC)** is the highest technical decision-making body of the GNU Radio Project.

Except where authority has been delegated by the TSC, the TSC has final authority over matters concerning the technical direction, development, maintenance, and stewardship of GNU Radio.

Each TSC member has one vote.

The responsibilities of the TSC include:

* establishing the overall technical direction of GNU Radio;  
* establishing and modifying technical project policies;  
* establishing, modifying, or retiring project components, repositories, working groups, and technical initiatives;  
* resolving technical questions that cannot be resolved through normal contributor and maintainer processes;  
* establishing policies governing maintainers and committers;  
* admitting new members of the TSC;  
*   
* appointing the TSC Chair;  
* delegating authority to the TSC Chair, maintainers, working groups, or other project bodies;  
* assign titles and positions to members of the TSC as needed;  
* reviewing decisions made under delegated authority; and  
* amending this technical governance document.

The TSC should delegate decisions to maintainers, working groups, and contributors whenever practical. Formal TSC authority is intended primarily for matters requiring project-wide coordination, policy, or resolution.

## **4\. TSC Membership**

TSC membership is held by individuals rather than companies, employers, or other organizations and is intended to represent **active stewardship of the GNU Radio Project**, not solely the right to participate in governance votes.

TSC members are expected to have demonstrated sustained commitment to GNU Radio and its community and maintain a meaningful role or area of responsibility within the project during their term.

Relevant contributions and responsibilities may include, among other things:

* software development;  
* Infrastructure maintenance and support;  
* serving as a maintainer or technical lead;  
* documentation;  
* testing and release activities;  
* leading or participating in a technical working group;  
* supporting releases, testing, documentation, or technical planning;  
* mentoring contributors or maintainers;  
* supporting community, education, outreach, or conference activities;  
* participating in project governance or operational activities;  
* representing the project in relevant technical communities, standards bodies, or external collaborations; or  
* taking responsibility for another area important to the health and continued development of GNU Radio.  
* project governance; and  
* other substantial contributions supporting the purposes of GNU Radio.

The purpose of this expectation is not to establish a quantitative contribution requirement or to privilege software development over other forms of contribution. Rather, TSC membership should reflect continuing responsibility for some aspect of the GNU Radio Project.

Annual reaffirmation of TSC membership represents both an intention to participate in TSC governance and a commitment to continue contributing to or accepting responsibility for some aspect of the GNU Radio Project.

TSC members are expected to participate constructively in project governance and uphold applicable GNU Radio and Linux Foundation policies, including the project's Code of Conduct.

TSC membership should not be maintained solely for the purpose of retaining voting rights.

## **5\. TSC Terms and Annual Reaffirmation**

TSC membership is for a term of **one year**.

At the end of each annual term, every TSC member must **positively affirm their intention to serve for the following term**.

Annual reaffirmation represents both an intention to participate in TSC governance and a commitment to continue fulfilling the expectations of active stewardship described in Section 5\.

A member who affirmatively elects to continue will begin a new one-year term without requiring re-election.

A member who does not affirmatively elect to continue by the established deadline will cease to be a TSC member at the expiration of their current term.

Annual reaffirmation is intended to preserve the broad representative character of the former General Assembly while ensuring that the TSC consists of individuals who continue to have an interest and stake in GNU Radio governance and stewardship.

The TSC will establish the procedures and schedule for annual reaffirmation.

There is no limit on the number of consecutive terms a TSC member may serve.

## **6\. Admission of New TSC Members**

The process for admitting new TSC members continues the membership principles of the GNU Radio General Assembly.

A candidate for TSC membership must:

1. be nominated by a current TSC member; and  
2. receive statements of support from at least two additional TSC members.

The primary consideration for admission should be the candidate's sustained commitment and contributions to GNU Radio and their demonstrated commitment to the project's community standards and Code of Conduct.

A nominee should ordinarily have an identifiable role, responsibility, or area of stewardship within the GNU Radio Project consistent with Section 5\.

Following nomination and the required statements of support, the TSC will vote on admitting the candidate.

A candidate is admitted upon approval by a simple majority in a valid TSC vote.

A new member admitted during an existing annual term serves until the end of that term and may then participate in the normal annual reaffirmation process.

## **7\. TSC Chair**

The TSC will appoint one of its members to serve as **TSC Chair**.

The responsibilities of the Chair include:

* facilitating meetings of the TSC;  
*   
* coordinating agendas;  
* ensuring that appropriate governance procedures are followed;  
* facilitating communication among maintainers, working groups, and the full TSC;  
* serving as a primary technical governance liaison between GNU Radio and the Linux Foundation;  
* ensuring that significant technical governance decisions are appropriately documented; and  
* performing additional duties delegated by the TSC.

The Chair is a facilitator and coordinator rather than an independent source of technical authority.

Except where authority is explicitly delegated by the TSC, the Chair has the same vote as every other TSC member.

The Chair does not possess a unilateral veto or tie-breaking vote.

## **8\. Chair Term**

Chair and appointments will normally be reviewed annually following the annual reaffirmation of TSC membership.

A Chair may serve consecutive terms.

A person must remain a member of the TSC to serve as Chair.

The TSC may replace the Chair before the expiration of their term.

## **9\. TSC Meetings**

The TSC will hold at least one real-time meeting each year.

Additional meetings may be held as necessary.

Meetings may occur in person or through electronic means that permit synchronous participation.  TSC meetings will ordinarily be open to the public. The TSC may enter a closed session when reasonably necessary for personnel, Code of Conduct, legal, security, or similarly confidential matters.

The TSC should provide reasonable advance notice of meetings at which substantive governance decisions will be considered.

Any TSC member may request that a matter be placed before the TSC.

The TSC may establish additional meeting procedures consistent with this document.

## **10\. TSC Decisions**

The TSC should seek consensus whenever practical.

When consensus cannot be reached or a formal decision is appropriate, each TSC member has one vote.

Unless otherwise specified in this document, a proposal is approved by a simple majority of votes cast.

A tie vote does not approve a proposal.

TSC decisions may be made during a real-time meeting or through an asynchronous electronic vote.

Procedures for electronic voting may be established separately by the TSC.

## **11\. Participation and Quorum**

Consistent with the historical governance of the GNU Radio General Assembly, a properly convened real-time TSC meeting may conduct business regardless of the number of TSC members participating.

The purpose of this provision is to prevent non-participation from preventing the project from conducting necessary governance.

This provision does not relieve TSC members of the expectation of active participation described elsewhere in this document.

Different participation or quorum requirements may be established for asynchronous votes.

The result of a voting only becomes effective when the number of votes which have chosen an option different from "Abstention" is greater than 20 percent of the number of active members, and the number of votes in total is greater or equal to 50 percent of the number of active members. If this quorum isn't reached the voting is considered invalid. An invalid voting can be repeated, but not before a period of four weeks has passed between publishing the results of the invalid vote and the proposal for the repeated voting.

## **12\. Asynchronous Voting**

The TSC may make decisions through asynchronous electronic votes.

The TSC may adopt voting procedures governing:

* notice;  
* discussion periods;  
* voting periods;  
* voting methods;  
* anonymity;  
* participation thresholds;  
* publication of results; and  
* administration of votes.

Until replaced by the TSC, the principles of the GNU Radio General Assembly's existing online voting procedures should be used as guidance where applicable.

## **13\. Removal and Resignation**

A TSC member may resign at any time.

Membership also terminates when a member does not affirmatively renew their membership at the end of their annual term.

The annual reaffirmation process is the preferred mechanism for allowing TSC membership to naturally reflect changes in an individual's involvement or responsibilities within the project.

However, a TSC member may be removed during an active term when they are no longer meaningfully participating in the project or fulfilling the expectations of active stewardship.

Grounds for removal may include:

* sustained failure to participate in TSC meetings, discussions, or votes;  
* prolonged lack of involvement in any identifiable project role or responsibility;  
* repeated failure to respond to reasonable attempts to contact the member regarding TSC or project responsibilities;  
* abandonment of an agreed project responsibility without reasonable communication;  
* serious violations of applicable governance documents or the project's Code of Conduct; or  
* other conduct materially inconsistent with the responsibilities of TSC membership.

Temporary periods of inactivity due to personal, professional, medical, or other reasonable circumstances should not ordinarily constitute grounds for removal when the member communicates their situation or continued intent to participate.

Before a member is removed for inactivity or non-participation, reasonable attempts should be made to contact the member and provide an opportunity for them to confirm their intent to remain active.

Removal requires approval by the TSC.

A member whose removal is being considered must be given reasonable notice and an opportunity to respond. The affected member may participate in discussion but may not vote on their own removal.

## **14\. Working Groups and Delegated Governance**

The TSC may establish working groups, committees, maintainer groups, release teams, or other technical bodies.

The TSC may delegate authority to these bodies.

Delegated authority remains subject to oversight by the TSC and may be modified or withdrawn by the TSC.

GNU Radio follows the principle that technical decisions should generally be made at the lowest practical level.

Routine decisions concerning a component should therefore ordinarily be made by the contributors and maintainers responsible for that component rather than by the Chair or full TSC.

The TSC should generally become involved when a matter:

* affects multiple project components;  
* cannot be resolved through normal maintainer processes;  
* establishes project-wide technical policy;  
* materially affects the technical direction of GNU Radio; or  
* requires formal governance action.

## **15\. Transparency**

Substantive decisions of the TSC will be documented.

Records should include, as appropriate:

* the matter considered;  
* the decision reached;  
* the result of any formal vote; and  
* relevant rationale.

Governance records should be made publicly available unless confidentiality is necessary because of personnel matters, Code of Conduct matters, legal requirements, security issues, or other appropriate reasons.

## **16\. Code of Conduct**

The GNU Radio Project will maintain a Code of Conduct

TSC members, maintainers, contributors, and participants in GNU Radio activities are expected to comply with the applicable Code of Conduct.

Code of Conduct enforcement procedures may be established separately and do not need to be exercised by the TSC unless specifically assigned to them.

## **17\. Amendments**

This governance document may be amended by the TSC.

Because changes to this document may materially alter the rights of TSC members or the structure of GNU Radio technical governance, amendments require approval of **three-fourths of the votes cast and affirmative votes from at least one-half of all currently seated TSC members**.

This threshold is intended to preserve the heightened level of agreement historically required for significant changes to GNU Radio's governing documents.

## **18\. Governance Continuity**

Where this document does not expressly establish a new procedure, the TSC should favor continuity with the governance principles and practices of the GNU Radio General Assembly.

The migration to the Linux Foundation is intended to change the organizational framework in which GNU Radio technical governance operates, not to unnecessarily replace the project's existing community-led governance culture.

In particular, the transition preserves the following principles:

* the project's highest technical governing body consists of individual community members;  
* each member has one vote;  
* sustained contribution and commitment are the basis for membership;  
* TSC membership represents active stewardship rather than passive voting rights;  
* new members are nominated and approved by existing members;  
* ordinary decisions are made by simple majority;  
* significant governance changes require broader consensus;  
* governance decisions are documented;  
* technical authority should be exercised at the lowest practical level; and  
* ultimate technical authority rests with the broader TSC rather than its smaller delegated leadership group.
